\begin{abstract}
Linear non-Gaussian cyclic structural causal models are observationally identifiable only up to a distributional equivalence class whose members differ by reversals of directed cycles \citep{lacerda2008discovering}. We show that this ambiguity disappears at the level of strongly connected components (SCCs): the \emph{condensation} $G'$ of the data-generating graph --- the DAG of SCCs together with their inter-SCC edges --- is invariant across the equivalence class and therefore identifiable from the observational distribution alone. Methodologically, we extend the coarsening lattice of \citet{wahl2024foundations,madaleno2026coarsening} to directed graphs with cycles, with the SCC partition as its structural floor and $G'$ as its finest acyclic quotient. Synthetic experiments confirm the result. Because $G'$ is a DAG, cluster-level causal effects are identifiable on it via existing C-DAG machinery \citep{anand2023causal}, providing a downstream payoff for the recovered structure.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
% =============================================================================

Causal discovery from observational data has predominantly focused on directed \emph{acyclic} graphs (DAGs). Yet many systems of scientific interest contain feedback loops: gene-regulatory networks \fransays{ref}, dynamical systems \fransays{ref}, economic equilibria \fransays{ref} all exhibit directed cycles. A separate line of work on \emph{cyclic} causal models has therefore developed, ranging from the structural-equation formulation of \citet{spirtes1995directed,bongers2021foundations} to the linear non-Gaussian models of \citet{lacerda2008discovering}, which exploit standard ICA \citep{eriksson2004identifiability} to recover the demixing matrix $W = I - B$ of a linear cyclic SCM up to row permutation and scaling, from which the weighted adjacency matrix $B$ and hence the cyclic graph $G$ can in principle be recovered.

The coarsening framework of \citet{wahl2024foundations, madaleno2026coarsening} characterises when a DAG can be simplified by grouping variables into clusters. We show that this framework extends naturally to directed graphs with cycles: the SCC partition emerges as a structural floor on the coarsening lattice --- the finest partition whose quotient is a DAG --- and the resulting cluster-DAG is exactly the \emph{condensation} $G'$ of graph theory (the DAG of SCCs together with their inter-SCC edges). This is the identification target that resolves the ambiguity in the equivalence class of \citet{lacerda2008discovering}.

\paragraph{Contributions.} We extend the coarsening framework to directed graphs that may contain cycles, and connect it to the cyclic-SCM identification literature. Concretely:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Condensation identifiability (\Cref{thm:condensation-id}, \S\ref{sec:approach-oica}).} The condensation $G'$ of a linear non-Gaussian cyclic SCM is invariant across the distributional equivalence class of \citet{lacerda2008discovering}, and is therefore identifiable from observational data alone, without invoking the stability and disjoint-cycles assumption of that work.
    \item \textbf{The SCC floor (\S\ref{sec:scc-floor}).} The coarsening lattice acquires a structural floor: any valid DAG-coarsening must group nodes in a common SCC, and the SCC partition is the finest valid coarsening. This is the structural reason why condensation identifiability holds.
    \item \textbf{Translating the distributional equivalence class of \citet{lacerda2008discovering} into a unique condensation $G'$}, thereby reducing causal inference in a cyclic system to causal inference in an acyclic one --- where the full machinery of DAG identification, such as the C-DAG identification theorems of \citet{anand2023causal}, applies.
\end{itemize}

The paper is organised as follows: \S\ref{sec:background} fixes notation and recalls the coarsening lattice and cyclic-SCM background; \S\ref{sec:cycles} introduces DAG-coarsenings of cyclic graphs, proves the SCC-floor proposition, presents the learning procedure, proves condensation identifiability and discusses the implication for cluster-level causal-effect identification; \S\ref{sec:experiments} reports the synthetic experiments; \S\ref{sec:discussion} concludes.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
% =============================================================================

\paragraph{Cyclic causal models.} Cyclic generalisations of structural causal models date back to \citet{spirtes1995directed}, with rigorous foundations in \citet{bongers2021foundations}. \citet{lacerda2008discovering} showed that linear non-Gaussian cyclic SCMs with no latent confounders can be identified via standard ICA: ICA recovers the demixing matrix $\hat W$, from which one obtains a candidate adjacency matrix $\hat B = I - \mathrm{diag}(P\hat W)^{-1} P\hat W$ for each admissible row permutation $P$, and thresholding yields the graph. Later work has extended this to latent-confounded settings via overcomplete ICA \citep{salehkaleybar2020learning, dai2026distributional}, group-wise extensions of LiNGAM \citep{maeda2020rcd}, and constraint-based methods using $\sigma$-separation \citep{forre2018constraint}.

\paragraph{Coarsening and abstraction of causal models.} Coarsening of causal models has been approached from several angles: causal abstraction in the structural-causal-model sense \citep{rubenstein2017causal,beckers2020approximate}, group-level structure under partial-order assumptions \citep{wahl2024foundations} and the partition-refinement approach of \citet{madaleno2026coarsening} that we extend here. Once a cluster-level causal graph (C-DAG) is in hand, the identification theorems of \citet{anand2023causal} characterise which cluster-level interventional distributions are recoverable from observational data, providing the natural downstream consumer of our identification result.

\paragraph{Strongly connected components and condensation.} The condensation construction is standard in graph theory; \citet{tarjan1972depth} gives a linear-time algorithm and \citet{gabow2000path} a path-based alternative. Our use of the SCC partition as the finest valid DAG-coarsening is, to our knowledge, new in the causal-discovery context: it transforms a cyclic model into an acyclic surrogate to which the standard DAG-identification machinery applies.

\section{Background}
\label{sec:background}
% =============================================================================

We recall the key definitions from \citet{madaleno2026coarsening}. A \emph{coarsening} of a DAG $G = (V, E)$ is a DAG $G' = (V', E')$ together with a surjection $\chi: V \to V'$ such that $E' = \{\chi(u) \to \chi(v) \mid u \to v \in E,\ \chi(u) \neq \chi(v)\}$. Equivalently, a coarsening is a partition $\Pi = \{\pi_1, \ldots, \pi_k\}$ of $V$ whose induced quotient graph is a DAG; we refer to the parts $\pi_i$ as \emph{clusters} throughout.

The set of all coarsenings of $G$, ordered by refinement, forms a sublattice of the partition refinement lattice (Theorem~3 in \citet{madaleno2026coarsening}). The \texttt{RePaRe} algorithm traverses this lattice using two oracles: \texttt{Refine} tests whether a cluster should be split into finer parts (refining the partition), and \texttt{IsEdge} tests whether two clusters are adjacent in the quotient (deciding inter-cluster edges via conditional-independence queries on cluster-level features).

When $G$ already contains cycles, the same construction gives a coarsening $G'$ provided the quotient is acyclic (\Cref{def:dag-coarsening} below). In the special case where the partition is the SCC partition of $G$, the resulting quotient is exactly the \emph{condensation} of $G$ from graph theory: the DAG whose vertices are the SCCs and whose edges record inter-SCC reachability. We use the same symbol $G'$ for the condensation and the cluster-DAG throughout.

\subsection{Cyclic structural causal models}
\label{sec:cyclic-scm}

A linear cyclic SCM is defined by:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sem}
    X = BX + E, \qquad \text{solved as } X = (I - B)^{-1} E,
\end{equation}
where $d = |V|$ is the number of variables, $B \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times d}$ is the weighted adjacency matrix of a directed graph $G = (V,E)$ (which may contain cycles), and the noise vector $E$ has mutually independent components. Following \citet{lacerda2008discovering}, the spectral radius of $B$ is $\rho(B) = \max_i |\lambda_i(B)|$, the largest absolute eigenvalue. The solution exists and is unique when $\rho(B) < 1$.

Two matrices play distinct roles. The \emph{mixing matrix} $A = (I - B)^{-1}$ maps independent sources $E$ to the observed $X = AE$; its support encodes the transitive closure of $G$ (i.e.\ total effects, reachability). The \emph{demixing matrix} $W = I - B = A^{-1}$ recovers sources from observations via $E = WX$; its off-diagonal support encodes the \emph{direct} adjacency of $G$.

We first generalise the notion of coarsening from DAGs to directed graphs that may contain cycles.

\begin{definition}[DAG coarsening of a directed graph]\label{def:dag-coarsening}
    Given a directed graph $G = (V, E)$ which may contain cycles, a \emph{DAG coarsening} of $G$ is a partition $\Pi = \{\pi_1, \ldots, \pi_k\}$ of $V$ together with a surjective mapping $\chi : V \to V'$ assigning each node to its cluster, such that the quotient graph $G' = (V', E')$ defined by
    \[
        E' = \{(\chi(u), \chi(v)) \mid (u, v) \in E,\ \chi(u) \neq \chi(v)\}
    \]
    is acyclic.
\end{definition}

When $G$ is a DAG this reduces to the original definition of \citet{madaleno2026coarsening}: the trivial singleton-per-node partition has an acyclic quotient, and so does any coarsening of it. For general directed graphs the acyclicity requirement is non-trivial and forces SCCs to be respected, as \Cref{prop:scc-floor} below shows.

\subsection{The SCC floor}
\label{sec:scc-floor}

\Cref{def:dag-coarsening} imposes a structural floor on the coarsening lattice.

\begin{proposition}[SCC floor]\label{prop:scc-floor}
    Let $G = (V, E)$ be a directed graph. Nodes $u, v \in V$ that belong to the same strongly connected component of $G$ must lie in the same cluster of any valid DAG-coarsening. Consequently, the SCC partition is the finest valid DAG-coarsening of $G$, and its quotient is the condensation $G'$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
    If $u$ and $v$ are in the same SCC, there exist directed paths $u \to \cdots \to v$ and $v \to \cdots \to u$ in $G$. If $\chi(u) \neq \chi(v)$ in a coarsening, these paths induce directed paths $\chi(u) \to \cdots \to \chi(v)$ and $\chi(v) \to \cdots \to \chi(u)$ in the quotient, forming a cycle and violating the DAG requirement.
\end{proof}

The SCC partition is computable in $O(|V| + |E|)$ via Tarjan's algorithm~\citep{tarjan1972depth}; for graphs with many SCCs Gabow's path-based algorithm can be modestly faster in practice~\citep{gabow2000path}.

\paragraph{Remark: cyclic coarsenings.}
\Cref{def:dag-coarsening} requires the coarsened graph to be a DAG, mirroring the original framework of \citet{madaleno2026coarsening}. A natural generalisation drops this requirement and asks only that the coarsening preserves the partial order over SCCs of $G$: any partition $\chi$ such that the quotient graph (a) does not reverse a non-cyclic ancestral relation in $G$, and (b) does not introduce a cycle between nodes that are not already in a common SCC, is a valid \emph{cyclic coarsening}. The interventional and observational coarsenings considered below are all valid DAG-coarsenings (and hence cyclic coarsenings), so we focus on the DAG case throughout. The general cyclic-coarsening lattice is interesting in its own right and we leave its characterisation to future work.

\begin{example}[example of \citet{lacerda2008discovering}]\label{ex:illustrative}
Consider the five-variable linear cyclic SCM
\[
    X_1 = E_1,\quad
    X_2 = 1.2\,X_1 - 0.3\,X_4 + E_2,\quad
    X_3 = 2\,X_2 + E_3,\quad
    X_4 = -X_3 + E_4,\quad
    X_5 = 3\,X_2 + E_5.
\]
The directed graph has edges $X_1\!\to\!X_2$, $X_2\!\to\!X_3$, $X_3\!\to\!X_4$, $X_4\!\to\!X_2$ (a 3-cycle), and $X_2\!\to\!X_5$ (\Cref{fig:example}a). The cycle $X_2\!\to\!X_3\!\to\!X_4\!\to\!X_2$ forms the unique non-trivial SCC $\{X_2,X_3,X_4\}$; nodes $X_1$ and $X_5$ are singletons. Since any DAG coarsening must absorb each SCC into a single cluster (\Cref{prop:scc-floor}), the finest valid coarsening has three clusters $\pi_1=\{X_1\}$, $\pi_2=\{X_2,X_3,X_4\}$, $\pi_3=\{X_5\}$, yielding the chain condensation $G' = \pi_1\to\pi_2\to\pi_3$ in \Cref{fig:example}b.
\end{example}

\paragraph{Combinatorial structure of the lattice.}
The full partition lattice of $n$ nodes is organised by partition count~$k$, with the number of $k$ partitions given by the Stirling numbers of the second kind $S(n, k)$. For $n = 5$:
\[
    S(5,1) = 1,\quad S(5,2) = 15,\quad S(5,3) = 25,\quad S(5,4) = 10,\quad S(5,5) = 1,
\]
yielding Bell number $B_5 = 52$ total partitions. For the graph of \Cref{ex:illustrative}, only 4 of these are valid DAG coarsenings: the single-cluster partition, the SCC floor $\{X_1\}\mid\{X_2,X_3,X_4\}\mid\{X_5\}$ at $k=3$, and the two intermediate 2-cluster coarsenings $\{X_1\}\mid\{X_2,X_3,X_4,X_5\}$ and $\{X_1,X_2,X_3,X_4\}\mid\{X_5\}$.

The remaining 48 partitions are invalid for two distinct reasons, illustrated in \Cref{fig:lattice}. Partitions that \emph{split} the SCC $\{X_2,X_3,X_4\}$ fail because the intra-SCC cycle $X_2\!\to\!X_3\!\to\!X_4\!\to\!X_2$ persists in the quotient (e.g.\ $\{X_1\}\mid\{X_2\}\mid\{X_3,X_4\}\mid\{X_5\}$). Partitions that \emph{merge across condensation layers} fail even though they keep the SCC intact: the 2-cluster partition $\{X_1,X_5\}\mid\{X_2,X_3,X_4\}$ is invalid because the edges $X_1\!\to\!X_2$ and $X_2\!\to\!X_5$ induce opposite-direction edges between the two clusters, creating a cycle in the quotient.

\subsection{Approach 1: Intervention-determined coarsening}
\label{sec:approach-ivn}

\fransays{Perhaps we can delete this?} Under soft interventions, the cyclic SCM becomes $X^{I} = (I-B)^{-1}(E + \delta)$, so intervention effects propagate through the entire graph, including back through cycles. Nodes within the same SCC are mutual ancestors and therefore share identical intervened-ancestor signatures: $\mathcal{I}\text{-an}_G(v) = \mathcal{I}\text{-an}_G(w)$ for all $v, w$ in the same SCC. Consequently, \texttt{RefineTest} can never split an SCC, and \texttt{RePaRe} recovers the interventional coarsening $G^{\mathcal{I}}$ with the SCC partition as its floor --- without any modification to the algorithm.


\subsection{Approach 2: Observational coarsening via linear non-Gaussian ICA}
\label{sec:approach-oica}

We assume the linear cyclic SCM of \eqref{eq:sem} with mutually independent, non-Gaussian noise terms $E_i$ and \emph{no latent confounders}, so the noise vector $E$ has the same dimension as $X$. Under these assumptions, standard ICA \citep{eriksson2004identifiability} recovers the demixing matrix $W = I - B$ up to row permutation and scaling. This is the regime exploited by \citet{lacerda2008discovering} for cyclic causal discovery; overcomplete ICA \citep{lewicki2000learning} is needed only when the number of independent sources exceeds the number of observed variables, which arises with latent confounders \citep{salehkaleybar2020learning, dai2026distributional}.

\paragraph{From demixing matrix to adjacency.}
Let $\hat W$ denote the ICA estimate. Each row permutation $P$ such that $P\hat W$ has nonzero diagonal entries yields an admissible candidate:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:B-from-W}
    \hat B_P \;=\; I \;-\; \mathrm{diag}(P\hat W)^{-1}\, P\hat W.
\end{equation}
The off-diagonal support of $\hat B_P$, after thresholding at $\tau$, gives a candidate directed graph $\hat G_P$. Different admissible permutations $P$ yield different members of the distributional equivalence class.

\paragraph{Distributional equivalence and cycle reversals.}
\citet{lacerda2008discovering} showed that linear non-Gaussian cyclic SCMs are observationally identifiable only up to a distributional equivalence class, whose members differ by reversals of directed cycles in $G$ (their Theorem~4). Every directed cycle lies within a single SCC (by definition of strong connectivity). Therefore cycle reversals modify only intra-SCC edges and leave all inter-SCC edges unchanged. The next theorem formalises the consequence.

\begin{theorem}[Condensation identifiability]\label{thm:condensation-id}
    Let $G_1, G_2$ be two directed graphs underlying linear cyclic SCMs of the form~\eqref{eq:sem}, both with no latent confounders, mutually independent noise terms and at most one Gaussian noise component. If they induce the same observational distribution over $X$, then their condensations are equal: $G_1' = G_2'$ (same SCC partition, same set of inter-SCC edges).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
    Equality of distributions places $G_1$ and $G_2$ in the same distributional equivalence class. By Theorem~4 of \citet{lacerda2008discovering}, the two graphs differ only by reversals of directed cycles.

    \emph{SCC partition.} Every directed cycle lies within a single SCC. Reversing a cycle within an SCC $\pi$ replaces each edge $u \to v$ on the cycle with $v \to u$; every node on the cycle retains a directed path to and from every other node, so all nodes in $\pi$ remain mutually reachable. Nodes outside $\pi$ are unaffected. Hence $G_1$ and $G_2$ have the same SCC partition.

    \emph{Inter-SCC edges.} An inter-SCC edge $u \to v$ lies on no directed cycle: a cycle through it would make $u$ and $v$ mutually reachable, hence in the same SCC. Cycle reversal therefore cannot touch any inter-SCC edge, and $G_1, G_2$ share the same inter-SCC edge set. Combined with the SCC equality above, this gives $G_1' = G_2'$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:condensation-id}
    In the large-sample limit, $G'$ is recoverable by running FastICA to obtain $\hat W$, enumerating admissible row permutations via an N-rooks search to obtain candidates $\hat B_P$ via~\eqref{eq:B-from-W}, picking any admissible representative $\hat B$, and applying Tarjan's algorithm to $\operatorname{supp}(\hat B)$. \Cref{thm:condensation-id} guarantees that the choice of representative does not affect the recovered condensation.
\end{corollary}

\fransays{We need to verify this properly, perhaps even prove it?} \paragraph{Faithfulness on the condensation.}
A second consequence of the SCC aggregation is the validity of conditional-independence tests at the cluster level.

\begin{lemma}[Faithfulness on the condensation]\label{lem:cond-faith}
    Under the linear cyclic SCM of~\eqref{eq:sem} with $\sigma$-faithfulness, the marginal joint distribution on cluster-level features is Markov and faithful to the condensation $G'$.
\end{lemma}

\noindent
The argument is standard: linear cyclic SCMs are $\sigma$-Markov to $G$ \citep{spirtes1995directed,bongers2021foundations}, $\sigma$-separation collapses to ordinary d-separation when the graph is acyclic, and aggregating variables to SCC clusters projects $\sigma$-separation statements about $G$ onto d-separation statements about the (acyclic) condensation. Under $\sigma$-faithfulness, no cancellations introduce extra independencies, so the projected distribution is Markov \emph{and} faithful to $G'$. In particular the same CI argument that justifies \texttt{IsEdge} in the original \texttt{RePaRe} carries over to inter-cluster features unchanged.

\paragraph{Algorithm.} This yields a procedure requiring only observational data --- ICA-LiNG-D \citep{lacerda2008discovering} composed with Tarjan, viewed through the coarsening lattice:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Estimate} $\hat W$ via FastICA \citep{hyvarinen1999fast} with as many components as variables.
    \item \textbf{Enumerate the equivalence class}: search row permutations $P$ such that $P\hat W$ has nonzero diagonal (an N-rooks problem); each yields a candidate $\hat B_P = I - \mathrm{diag}(P\hat W)^{-1}\,P\hat W$ via~\eqref{eq:B-from-W}. Threshold $|\hat B_P|$ at $\tau$. Optionally filter to the \emph{stable} candidates with $\rho(\hat B_P) < 1$.
    \item \textbf{Pick any admissible representative} $\hat B$. By \Cref{thm:condensation-id} the condensation does not depend on the choice; \textbf{recover the partition} $\hat\Pi$ as the SCCs of $\operatorname{supp}(\hat B)$ via Tarjan's algorithm \citep{tarjan1972depth} and \textbf{the condensation edges} as the inter-cluster entries of $\operatorname{supp}(\hat B)$.
\end{enumerate}
\Cref{cor:condensation-id} guarantees consistency of this procedure in the large-sample limit.

\paragraph{Conservative behaviour at the partition step.} Estimation noise can cause entries of $\hat B$ to fall below $\tau$ and \emph{merge} distinct SCCs into a single cluster, but it can never \emph{split} a true SCC: a coarser-than-necessary partition is still a valid DAG-coarsening, whereas splitting an SCC would induce a cycle in the quotient graph and violate the DAG requirement.

\subsection{Causal Effect Identification on the Condensation}
\label{sec:effects}
% =============================================================================

\Cref{thm:condensation-id} elevates the recovered structure from a partition into a fully oriented C-DAG over clusters. Because the condensation is acyclic, the standard machinery of cluster-level causal-effect identification applies.

\fransays{explain this further}
\paragraph{Cluster-level interventions in cyclic SCMs.} Hard interventions on a single variable break the cyclic mechanism (\S\ref{sec:background}) and are excluded throughout the paper. In contrast, a hard intervention on an \emph{entire SCC} simultaneously is well-defined: replacing all equations within a cluster $\pi$ with constants yields $X^{\,\mathrm{do}(\pi = c)} = (I_{V \setminus \pi} - B_{V \setminus \pi})^{-1} (E_{V \setminus \pi} + B_{V \setminus \pi,\,\pi}\, c)$, and the surrounding cyclic substructure outside $\pi$ remains consistent under $\rho(B) < 1$. Soft (shift) interventions on cluster members are likewise well-defined and, applied uniformly across $\pi$, induce a cluster-level shift on $G'$.

\paragraph{C-DAG identification.} Once the condensation $G'$ is in hand, the C-DAG identification theorems of \citet{anand2023causal} apply directly: for clusters $\pi_i, \pi_j$ in $G'$, the cluster-level interventional distribution $P(\pi_j \mid \mathrm{do}(\pi_i = c))$ is identifiable from $P(X)$ whenever the standard ID conditions hold on $G'$. We do not introduce a new ID algorithm; the contribution is to observe that the condensation is the natural object on which existing C-DAG ID machinery operates, and that \Cref{thm:condensation-id} supplies its prerequisite from purely observational data, even though the underlying micro-graph $G$ is itself not identifiable.

\section{Experimental Results}
\label{sec:experiments}
% =============================================================================

\alexsays{maybe worth adding the the structural baseline from anne's paper: \url{https://proceedings.mlr.press/v286/petersen25a.html}}

We illustrate \Cref{thm:condensation-id} on synthetic linear non-Gaussian cyclic SCMs using the recipe of \citet{lacerda2008discovering} from \S\ref{sec:approach-oica}. The intervention-determined approach (\S\ref{sec:approach-ivn}) is theoretically covered by the unmodified guarantees of \citet{madaleno2026coarsening} and is not explored here.

\subsection{Setup}

\paragraph{Data-generating process.} We generate linear non-Gaussian cyclic SCMs on $d = 10$ variables with a controlled number $\kappa \in \{3, 4, 5\}$ of non-trivial SCCs and edge densities $\lambda \in \{0.3, 0.5, 0.8\}$. For each $(d, \kappa, \lambda)$, the $d$ nodes are randomly partitioned into $\kappa$ non-trivial SCCs (each of size $\geq 2$) plus singleton nodes. Within each SCC a directed Hamilton cycle ensures mutual reachability and additional intra-SCC directed edges are sampled at probability $\lambda$. Between clusters, edges that respect a random topological order are sampled at probability $\lambda$. Edge weights are sampled in $[0.5, 0.95]$ with random sign. Noise terms $E_i$ are mutually independent and Laplace-distributed. Sample sizes range over $n \in \{50, 100, 500, 1000, 5000, 10000, 50000, 100000\}$; each cell is replicated across 10 random seeds.

\paragraph{Regimes.} We run each configuration under two spectral-radius regimes:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Stable} ($\rho(B) < 1$). Edge weights are rescaled so that $\rho(B) \approx 0.9$, close to the stability boundary but satisfying the condition required by \citet{lacerda2008discovering}. The first-stable filter can in principle recover the true $B$.
    \item \textbf{Unstable} ($\rho(B) > 1$). The same weight pool is rescaled to target $\rho(B) = 1.5$. The true $B$ violates the stability condition, so the first-stable filter does not necessarily select the correct member of the distributional equivalence class. By \Cref{thm:condensation-id}, the coarsening is nevertheless the same.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Method.} We run ICA-LiNG-D \citep{lacerda2008discovering}: FastICA estimates $\hat W$, admissible row permutations are enumerated via N-rooks search, and each yields a candidate $\hat B_P = I - \mathrm{diag}(P\hat W)^{-1}\, P\hat W$ via~\eqref{eq:B-from-W}. Candidates are thresholded at $\tau = 0.1$ and partitioned into stable ($\rho(\hat B) < 1$) and unstable sets. We take the first stable candidate and read off the SCC partition (Tarjan on $\operatorname{supp}(\hat B)$) and the condensation edges. By \Cref{cor:condensation-id} this is consistent for the condensation in the large-sample limit.

\paragraph{Metrics.} Ground truth is the true SCC partition of $G$ (Tarjan on the data-generating $B$) and the corresponding condensation. We report:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \emph{ARI} of the predicted partition versus the true SCC partition;
    \item \emph{cluster-DAG $F_1$}: $F_1$ of predicted variable-level edges projected onto the true SCC partition, compared to the true coarsening edges --- the identifiable target of \Cref{thm:condensation-id};
    \item \emph{variable-level $F_1$}: $F_1$ over all directed edges (including intra-SCC), to quantify the gap between identifiable and unidentifiable structure.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Results}

\Cref{fig:results} shows partition and edge recovery as functions of sample size for both regimes and all three densities, faceted by $\kappa$. Three observations:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \emph{Partition recovery (ARI).} ARI rises with $n$ and approaches 1 in both regimes and at all densities, confirming consistency of LiNG-D + Tarjan under non-Gaussian noise. Below $n \approx 500$ partitions are noisy.
    \item \emph{Cluster-DAG $F_1$.} Cluster-DAG $F_1$ converges to 1 with $n$ in both the stable and unstable regimes, directly illustrating \Cref{thm:condensation-id}: the condensation is identifiable regardless of whether the data-generating $B$ satisfies the stability condition.
    \item \emph{Variable-level $F_1$.} In the stable regime (solid lines), the first-stable filter recovers the true $B$ and variable-level $F_1 \to 1$. In the unstable regime (dashed lines), the true $B$ has $\rho(B) = 1.5$, so the filter necessarily picks a different equivalence-class member; variable-level $F_1$ saturates strictly below 1. The gap is the unidentifiable intra-SCC structure.
\end{itemize}
The contrast between the two regimes is the key empirical message: the condensation (rows 1--2) is robustly recovered even when the variable-level graph (row 3) is not. This is the practical motivation for \S\ref{sec:effects}: rather than chasing the unidentifiable intra-SCC structure, work at the cluster level, where the C-DAG is fully recovered and standard C-DAG identification machinery applies.

\subsection{Scalability vs disjointCycles}
\label{sec:scalability}

We compare our procedure against \emph{disjointCycles} \citep{drton2024disjointcycles}, a cycle-aware baseline that estimates a topological ordering of singletons and cycles using higher-order moment tests (rather than ICA) and is explicitly designed to scale to large $d$ under the assumption that cycles in the true graph are disjoint. This is exactly the setting of \Cref{thm:condensation-id}, so disjointCycles is the natural head-to-head baseline at the SCC level.

\paragraph{Setup.} We sweep $d \in \{20, 50, 100, 500\}$ at fixed $\kappa = 10$ non-trivial SCCs, $\rho(B) \approx 0.9$ (stable regime), edge density $\lambda = 0.5$, and noise distribution Laplace, with $n \in \{50, 100, 500, 1000, 5000\}$. We restrict the grid to $n \geq d$: ICA-LiNG-D requires at least as many samples as features for the unmixing matrix to be well-defined, so cells with $n < d$ are mathematically ill-posed for both methods and are omitted. Both methods are run with significance level $\alpha = 0.01$ (for disjointCycles, applied uniformly to $\alpha_2, \alpha_3, \alpha_R$ with Holm correction and $\chi^2$ root-pruning, matching the setup of \citealt{drton2024disjointcycles}). We report the median over 10 seeds with 95\% bootstrap CI bands.

\paragraph{Results (\Cref{fig:scalability}).} Across all $d$, ARI and cluster-DAG $F_1$ for our method rise toward 1 with $n$, with the curve shifting rightward only mildly as $d$ grows --- the partition remains identifiable at $d = 500$ once $n$ is in the thousands. disjointCycles tracks our method on the partition metric at small $d$ but degrades faster as $d$ grows, reflecting the accumulating multiple-testing burden of its layer-by-layer hypothesis tests. The runtime panel (bottom row, log scale) shows our method's FastICA + N-rooks pipeline scaling favourably with $d$: fit time at $d = 500$ is comparable to disjointCycles at $d = 100$.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
% =============================================================================

We have shown that the coarsening framework of \citet{madaleno2026coarsening} extends naturally to directed graphs with cycles, with the SCC partition as the lattice floor and the condensation $G'$ as the canonical identification target. The central theorem (\Cref{thm:condensation-id}) sharpens the cyclic-SCM identifiability picture of \citet{lacerda2008discovering}: although the underlying micro-graph is identifiable only up to a distributional equivalence class, its condensation is fully identifiable from the observational distribution. This identification result is what enables applying C-DAG causal-effect identification \citep{anand2023causal} to the recovered structure.

The observational approach requires linear non-Gaussian noise and no latent confounders --- a stronger assumption than the Gaussianity sufficient for the intervention-determined approach, but with the substantial practical advantage of needing no interventions at all. Hard (do) interventions on a single variable, which sever the cyclic mechanism, are excluded throughout; only soft (shift) interventions and hard interventions on entire SCCs are well-defined in the cyclic SCM setting (\S\ref{sec:effects}).

\paragraph{Open directions.} (i) Latent-confounder generalisations via overcomplete ICA \citep{salehkaleybar2020learning,dai2026distributional}: the cycle-reversal argument carries over because cycle reversals are purely a graph-level operation, independent of the source/observed dimension. (ii) Mixed-Gaussian extension --- the argument survives intra-SCC Gaussian noise as long as at most one source per SCC is Gaussian, which would address the open problem of \citet[\S6]{lacerda2008discovering} on Gaussian/non-Gaussian mixtures. (iii) An empirical realisation of cluster-level causal-effect estimation on the recovered C-DAG, beyond the theoretical bridge sketched in \S\ref{sec:effects}. (iv) Characterising the cyclic-coarsening lattice (the broader generalisation noted in \S\ref{sec:scc-floor}) and identifying which of its elements are recoverable from data.

\section{Proofs}
\label{app:proofs}
% =============================================================================

For a directed graph $G = (V, E)$, denote the existence of a directed path from vertex $u$ to vertex $v$ by the reachability relation $u \leadsto_G v$. By convention, a vertex can always reach itself (paths of length 0), so $u \leadsto_G u$ for all $u \in V$.

\begin{definition}[Strongly connected component]
\label{def:scc}
Define a binary relation $\sim_G$ on $V$ by
\[
u \sim_G v \iff (u \leadsto_G v) \land (v \leadsto_G u).
\]
The relation $\sim_G$ is an equivalence relation (reflexive, symmetric, transitive). A \emph{strongly connected component} (SCC) of $G$ is an equivalence class of $V$ under $\sim_G$.
\end{definition}

\subsection{Transitive closure and SCCs}
\label{app:transitive-closure}

The following result provides an alternative route to condensation identifiability via the mixing matrix $A = (I-B)^{-1}$ and its transitive-closure interpretation. While the main proof of \Cref{thm:condensation-id} uses the more direct cycle-reversal argument, the transitive-closure perspective is independently useful --- for instance, it connects to the path-rank lemma of \citet{dai2026distributional} and to settings where one works with $A$ rather than $W$.

\begin{definition}[Transitive closure]
\label{def:transitive-closure}
The \emph{transitive closure} of a directed graph $G = (V, E)$ is the graph $G^* = (V, E^*)$ with edge set
\[
E^* = \{ (u, v) \in V \times V \mid u \leadsto_G v \}.
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[SCCs are preserved under transitive closure]
\label{thm:scc-tc}
Let $G = (V, E)$ be a directed graph and let $G^* = (V, E^*)$ be its transitive closure. Then $G$ and $G^*$ have the same SCCs.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
It suffices to show $u \sim_G v \iff u \sim_{G^*} v$ for all $u, v \in V$.

\noindent($\Rightarrow$) Suppose $u \sim_G v$, so $u \leadsto_G v$ and $v \leadsto_G u$. By \Cref{def:transitive-closure}, $(u, v) \in E^*$ and $(v, u) \in E^*$, hence $u \leadsto_{G^*} v$ and $v \leadsto_{G^*} u$, i.e.\ $u \sim_{G^*} v$.

\noindent($\Leftarrow$) Suppose $u \sim_{G^*} v$, so there is a path $u = x_0, x_1, \dots, x_k = v$ in $G^*$. Each step $(x_i, x_{i+1}) \in E^*$ implies $x_i \leadsto_G x_{i+1}$ by \Cref{def:transitive-closure}. Transitivity of $\leadsto_G$ then gives $u \leadsto_G v$. Symmetric reasoning yields $v \leadsto_G u$, so $u \sim_G v$.

The two implications give equality of equivalence classes, hence equality of SCCs.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Remark.} \Cref{thm:scc-tc} means that if one recovers $\operatorname{supp}(A) = \operatorname{adjacency}(G^*)$ --- the transitive closure of $G$ --- from the mixing matrix $A = (I-B)^{-1}$, the SCC partition extracted from it is exactly the SCC partition of $G$. This provides an alternative proof route for the SCC-partition part of \Cref{thm:condensation-id}, going through $A$ rather than $W$. The main text uses the cycle-reversal argument because it also immediately handles inter-SCC edges without requiring a separate step.
